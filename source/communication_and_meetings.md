# Communication and meetings

## In-person meetings

### Individual meetings

- Every week, typically Mondays, scheduled via Outlook calendar
- Prepare in advance:
  - What are upcoming events (2-3 months; e.g. committee meeting, conference, meetings with collaborators)
  - Progress and results
  - Other activities (including non-scientific like courses attended, tutorials, infrastructure)
  - Next goals (list next steps in research and what tasks are needed to accomplish them)
  - Roadblocks/needs (list critical needs that are/you anticipate are blocking progress - especially things you need André to do. e.g. setup a VM, learn about a framework, download lots of data, more space in cluster)
  - Literature (indicate a paper you found interesting/relevant that André should read or that should be discussed in a journal club)
  - The above can be shared with André in advance (e.g. in a markdown file)
- The meta layer / check-in:
  - Talk about how you are doing, progress, frustration, goals beyond research
  - Once per month, more often if needed
- The scheduled meetings should not prevent you from reaching out to André at any time if needed (in person, via email, chat, phone)!

### Lab meetings

- Wednesdays at 09:00, meeting room at CeMM or LBI-NetMed - scheduled via Outlook calendar
- Schedule:
  - The schedule for the presentations is set by André and available [here](https://cemmat.sharepoint.com/:x:/r/sites/rendeirolab/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7BCA81B64C-2577-459B-BCAB-89C6678B342B%7D).
- Format:
  - Housekeeping (announcements, scheduling)
    - Any update relevant to the lab by anyone
  - One scheduled presentation (30-45 min) including:
    - Scientific question/hypothesis
    - Background (what has been done/can we build on)
    - Approach/methodology
    - Results
    - Outlook/next steps
  - Quick round reviewing week progress (20 min max, 3-5 min per person, optional):
    - What did you try to achieve? (broadly and specifically)
    - What were the challenges? How can we help?

### Code review

- Every 2 weeks (to begin, then maybe less frequent)
- 1h30 to 2h00 duration
- The goal is to improve everyone's programming skills/process - not shaming!
- Typical workflow:
  - Nominate one script/function to study (e.g. caused problems or is inefficient, not Pythonic, etc...) OR ask André beforehand to look at a repository to select one piece of code to focus on.
  - Ideally only 200-400 lines of code at one time
  - Everyone studies the goals of the code and the implementation
  - Together try to assess:
    1. Readability and documentation
    1. Architecture
    1. Re-usability
    1. Additional: packaging, speed, security, etc...
  - End by highlighting one new GitHub repository that you find cool/useful for the group - everyone quickly installs and explores it
  - Occasionally/alternatively we can talk about the meta part: cluster issues, installation, text editors, .bashrc setup, etc...

### Hackathons

- One afternoon every 2-3 months
- Goals:
  - Learn something new
  - Have fun
  - Do something useful for the community (CeMM, Vienna, world)
- Non-goals:
  - Win
  - Do everything
  - Do something perfect

  - Values

  - Feedback

  - Jointly revisit the [code of conduct](code_of_conduct.md)

## Lab retreats

Once a year, usually in early September, the whole lab goes on a 2-3 day retreat outside Vienna (e.g. Gumpoldskirchen in 2025, Dürnstein in the Wachau in 2026), travelling together by train.

- Organization:
  - Lab members volunteer to organize the retreat: they choose the dates and venue a few months in advance and share a program with the sessions and schedule.
  - Fill in the CeMM travel application form before the retreat.
  - The venue needs a meeting room with a projector, internet and a whiteboard or flipchart.
- Typical sessions:
  - **Funding update**: the PI presents the lab's grants, what is on track, and where more focus is needed, followed by discussion.
  - **Presenting projects in new ways**: e.g. swapping short presentations and giving each other's talk, or re-framing your own project for a different audience or story.
  - **Paper showdown**: everyone brings 1-3 cutting-edge papers bringing new perspectives to the lab (1 min each).
  - **Creative ideation and project pitches**: small teams develop wild new project ideas and pitch them to the group for feedback.
  - **New directions for the lab**: discussion of themes, projects and technologies for the next 3-5 years, and organizational changes needed.
  - **Lab life**: career development round table, sharing failure stories, and how to improve communication and productivity.
  - **Wrap-up**: action points with owners and deadlines.
- Social activities: dinners out, a hike, and evening games or music jam.

Prepare any presentations or papers requested in the program before the retreat.
